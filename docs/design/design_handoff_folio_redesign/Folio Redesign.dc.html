Two Rooms, One Shelf

Library is one unbroken contact-sheet grid: uniform crops, 2px gutters, no captions, sorted by capture date. An Event breaks that grid on purpose — a titled cover strip, loose date clusters with mono timestamps, occasional uncropped triptych rows, contributor initials stamped on shared frames.

Distinct because: the difference is carried entirely by grid rhythm and metadata density — no "shared" badge, no icon.

A Catalog, Not a Grid

Each style owns its own print idiom rather than a shared card shell with a different photo: Wedding is engraved serif on an ivory mat and takes the cover-feature slot; Polaroid is a literal polaroid frame; Travel borrows passport-stamp mono type and a compass mark; Adventure sits on black like a contact strip with sprocket holes.

Distinct because: crop the photo out of any card and you'd still know which style it was.

The Table, Not the Toolbar

A 64px page rail replaces thumbnails-in-a-drawer. The top strip carries only a technical stamp — spread number, pixel dimensions, DPI, autosave time — no icon row. The right rail is a properties drawer that appears with 3–4 fields max once something's selected; the canvas gets everything else.

Distinct because: chrome matches what a darkroom technician would actually keep at a light table, not a permanent shadcn toolbar.

The Album, on the Table

The album sits at an angle on a dark stage with a real cast shadow, framed by registration crosses at all four corners like a press proof. A mono spec stamp (trim size, spread count, stock) sits where a lab would actually stamp it; the order button reads as a rubber stamp, hard-edged, not rounded.

Distinct because: the decoration IS the production marks — no generic spinning card in a gradient void.

A Commission, Not a Ticket

The artist leads, styled like a gallery didactic — portrait, name, a one-line credential, a contact-sheet strip of past work — before any input field appears. The brief is one flowing serif prompt, not boxed fields. Turnaround reads as a lab ticket stamp, not a tooltip.

A Masthead and Two Stamps

Profile reads as a magazine masthead: name in serif, a mono credit line beneath. The general share card keeps the paper field and bleeds a hero photo on three edges. The occasion card is the one place the accent runs as a full field — terracotta ground, photo set small and centered like a locket, a single registration cross as the only ornament.

Distinct because: the same corner cross and mono credit line repeat on every card, so any occasion card reads as Folio's before the wordmark does.
